👹 Room 1 — monster

A rattling skeleton patrol holds the room (attack 10, health 23). The party must decide how to get past it. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. A pit takes up a third of the floor. The bottom is not visible. A font holds water that has been still a long time and is somehow clean.

They might have chosen to speak with whatever wears the armour, or strip the insignia off it — Michael Maier made the case: "Add nothing until you know what it does. Then add plenty." The party chose to bless the font and drink.

⛲ The cleric says the words over a stone font of still water and the party drinks: 12 health healed.

🛡️ The order settles with John Napier at the head of it: whatever comes, comes to them.

🕯️ Room 2 — shrine

A shrine. Resting here heals the party; the gold leaf on the altar could be stripped instead. A font holds water that has been still a long time and is somehow clean.

They might have chosen to sing the third harmony, or attune the resonances — Giordano Bruno made the case: "Overchannel? I call it generous casting." The party chose to link the three frequencies.

🔗 The three principles understand each other. Tension dissolves; the frequencies fade into silence, and the walls steady. +16 score. 🎓 Everything this asks for, the party happens to have brought: +25 renown. 🗝️ Whoever built this had habits, and the party has just learned one of them.

⚗️ Room 3 — lab

An alchemy lab with a working bench. An alchemist with materials can brew or coat weapons here. Somebody stacked supplies here and never came back for them. A brazier burns in its bracket — nobody has been here to feed it, and it burns anyway.

They might have chosen to experiment with the assembly, or dissolve the lock — Marsilio Ficino made the case: "Grace favors the well-prepared." The party chose to crack the crates open.

📦 The party cracks open stacked crates and barrels: 40 gold, 2 materials.

⚠️ Room 4 — trap

A trap blocks the corridor. The party must disarm it, avoid it, or take the hit. A pit takes up a third of the floor. The bottom is not visible. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around.

They might have chosen to search for a way around, or bypass it with a utility spell — John Napier made the case: "I hit hardest from the side nobody's watching." The party chose to push through and take the hit.

💥 The party pushes through the trap, taking 4 damage. 🪶 Feather Step takes the party's weight off the floor: 3 less damage from anything underfoot.

🪜 Room 5 — stairs

The floor ends at a stairwell. Whatever is below has been waiting longer.

They might have chosen to take the stair down, or camp at the stairhead first — Michael Maier made the case: "If it smokes, it works. If it screams, it works better." The party chose to rope down the shaft beside it.

🪜 The party ropes down the shaft beside the stair and lands on the second floor. 🪢 The line goes down the shaft beside the stair: the party descends without burning a march of oil.

🌿 Room 6 — materials

A room of herbs, salts, and quicksilver — alchemy materials, free to gather. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around. A boulder sits at the top of a slope, held by a wedge of rotten timber.

They might have chosen to search the room methodically, or divine what is hidden here — Giordano Bruno made the case: "The safety margin is where the good magic lives." The party chose to sift the rubble.

🪨 The party sifts a fall of rubble: 25 gold, 2 materials.

🕯️ Someone checks the reservoir and does not like the answer: oil for 2 more marches.

🌋 Room 7 — disaster

The dungeon itself turns hostile. The party must brace together or scatter. A pit takes up a third of the floor. The bottom is not visible. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around.

They might have chosen to speak mind to mind, or send something under the slab — Marsilio Ficino made the case: "The god forgives. That's the whole strategy." The party chose to pass a plan through the stone.

📡 The link carries more than reassurance: a plan goes across it, timed, with both halves moving on the same count. They meet in the middle of whatever was waiting, from two sides at once, and are still in that shape when the next room opens. +36 score. 🎓 It is answered from three directions at once and stays answered: +25 renown. 🗝️ And the shape of the place gives itself away: something sealed below will open to this.

🕯️ The lantern is burning low: oil for 1 more march.

📚 Room 8 — library

A library. The party can study here to learn spells. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind. Shelves sag under books nobody has audited in a century.

They might have chosen to question the ghost gently, or scry for the map — John Napier made the case: "Everything down here is carrying something. I collect." The party chose to reconstruct his rounds.

🧠 He is asked to describe his rounds, all of them, and somebody holds the whole account at once until the shape closes. The map was behind the shelving. Two marches of oil saved. +28 score. 🎓 Three disciplines on one problem, none of them guessing: +25 renown.

🕯️ The lantern dies with the party still under the hill. No more light to carry.

🐉 Room 9 — boss

The boss chamber. The Dragon Whelp of the Deep Vault waits at its center (attack 23, health 66); killing it clears the dungeon. It flinches from the cold: weak to frost (frost damage ×1.5). A silvered mirror leans against the wall, and it shows the room more honestly than the room does. A brazier burns in its bracket — nobody has been here to feed it, and it burns anyway.

They might have chosen to stand and fight, or fall back — the Cunning had already worked out the odds. The party chose to shove it into the brazier.
