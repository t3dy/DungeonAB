The transcript

🕯️ Room 1 — shrine

A shrine. Resting here heals the party; the gold leaf on the altar could be stripped instead. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind. A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong.

They might have chosen to diagnose and treat the fever, or apply a natural remedy — Cornelius Agrippa made the case: "Plan? I'm the plan." The party chose to bring the fever down.

💚 The fever breaks under the working's touch. Cornelius Agrippa is steady again. +15 score.

⚠️ Room 2 — trap

A trap blocks the corridor. The party must disarm it, avoid it, or take the hit. A pit takes up a third of the floor. The bottom is not visible. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around.

They might have chosen to disarm the trap, or search for a way around — Robert Fludd made the case: "I'll scout it — from inside." The party chose to push through and take the hit.

💥 The party pushes through the trap, taking 5 damage.

🛡️ Cornelius Agrippa takes the front of the march, and the rest fall in behind.

✚ That one leaves a mark on Cornelius Agrippa. Their ceiling drops to 12 for the rest of the delve. The Reckless do not stop to bind anything, and more of it stays with them. The Devout tend what the dungeon opens: fewer blows leave a mark that stays.

👹 Room 3 — monster

A chittering cloud of castle bats holds the room (attack 3, health 7). The party must decide how to get past it. It is a swarm: spell openings hit it ×1.5. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind. A crack runs the width of the room, wide enough to matter and too wide to jump twice.

They might have chosen to stand and fight, or fall back — John Napier made the case: "If it bleeds on me, that's how I know it's working." The party chose to fight from behind the pillars.

🏛️ The party backs into a row of squat pillars and makes the monster come down one aisle at a time: 3 damage as it closes. ⚔️ A chittering cloud of castle bats is finished before it can strike back. 🗡️ The daggers land before the argument starts: nothing comes back in the first round. 💥 The Reckless finish the fight with style: +5 score. 🎒 4 more preparations hold besides — the ledger keeps them.

🚶 The party looks into the burial wing (burial niches, most of them open) and keeps to the main route.

💰 Room 4 — treasure

A treasure chest sits in the room. It may hold gold; it may be a mimic. A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong. A silvered mirror leans against the wall, and it shows the room more honestly than the room does.

They might have chosen to experiment with the assembly, or dissolve the lock — John Dee made the case: "Faith walks in front. So do I." The party chose to loot the treasure.

💰 The party loots the chest: 21 gold.

🪐 Room 5 — situation

A connecting corridor. Nothing blocks the way; the party moves through.

They might have chosen to appraise the three chests, or pick the chest that was opened before — Cornelius Agrippa made the case: "My shield has a saint on it. She's watching. Form up." The party chose to read the maker's mark.

🏺 The mark of Maestro Cellini seals the chest — his work alone was worth the journey. +35 gold, +20 score. 🎓 Done by people who do something near it: 10 renown less than a clean job.

📚 Room 6 — library

A library. The party can study here to learn spells. A font holds water that has been still a long time and is somehow clean. A brazier burns in its bracket — nobody has been here to feed it, and it burns anyway.

They might have chosen to imagine what the image should be, or recognize the mosaic — Robert Fludd made the case: "I disarm faster when it's already ticking." The party chose to reconstruct the mosaic from memory.

🧠 Every detail aligns. The mosaic rebuilds perfectly, and what it shows is the floor plan of the rooms ahead — where the snare is, and where there is room to spread out before it. +20 score. 🎓 Near enough to the discipline to try, not near enough to be paid for it: 10 renown short.

🪐 Room 7 — situation

A connecting corridor. Nothing blocks the way; the party moves through.

They might have chosen to speak with whatever wears the armour, or name the armour by its heraldry — John Napier made the case: "The body is a temple. Mine's a fortress." The party chose to strip the insignia off it.

🗡️ The binding is three rivets and a sealed strip of vellum. Out they come, and the plate is only plate again — with a strip of very old vellum worth keeping. +8 gold, +20 score. 🎓 It is answered from three directions at once and stays answered: +25 renown.

🕯️ The lantern is burning low: oil for 2 more marches.

💰 Room 8 — treasure

A treasure chest sits in the room. It may hold gold; it may be a mimic. A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong. A silvered mirror leans against the wall, and it shows the room more honestly than the room does.

They might have chosen to open it with Knock, or inspect it first — John Dee made the case: "The light goes first. I merely follow it, loudly." The party chose to loot the treasure.

🦷 The chest is a mimic. It bites for 5 damage before the party kills it, recovering 11 gold.

🕯️ The lantern is burning low: oil for 1 more march.

✚ 2 wounds on Cornelius Agrippa, and the ceiling with them: 10, and no more.

The boss chamber. The Vampire Lord, apologizing for the hour waits at its center (attack 11, health 29); killing it clears the dungeon. It is ethereal: weapons do only 60% damage unless a cleric blesses the blades. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind. A crack runs the width of the room, wide enough to matter and too wide to jump twice.
