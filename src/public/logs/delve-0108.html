⚠️ Room 1 — trap

A trap blocks the corridor. The party must disarm it, avoid it, or take the hit. Dead beetles ring one tile: a poison trap. Light damage now, lingering venom later unless a cleric cures it. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around. A bed of rusted spikes stands out of the floor, most of them still upright.

They might have chosen to solve the progression, or cross in order — Johannes Trithemius made the case: "Doors, guards, promises — all pickable." The party chose to push through and take the hit.

💥 The party pushes through the trap, taking 1 damage. 🪶 Feather Step takes the party's weight off the floor: 3 less damage from anything underfoot. 🐍 The needles hit, but the cleric cures the venom on the spot.

🛡️ The order settles with Cornelius Agrippa at the head of it: whatever comes, comes to them. 🚶 The party looks into the barracks wing (bunkrooms and a picked-over weapon rack) and keeps to the main route. 🕳️ The floor gives way — a hidden trapdoor. The party lands on the second floor, 5 rooms past where they were, taking 3 damage, and the rooms between go unlooted.

🕯️ Room 2 — shrine

A shrine. Resting here heals the party; the gold leaf on the altar could be stripped instead. A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind.

They might have chosen to strip the gold leaf, or move on — Marsilio Ficino made the case: "Heal fast, ask later." The party chose to rest and heal.

🕯️ The party rests at the shrine: 5 health healed per member.

👹 Room 3 — monster

A barrow shade, thin as smoke holds the room (attack 15, health 21). The party must decide how to get past it. It is ethereal: weapons do only 60% damage unless a cleric blesses the blades. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind. A silvered mirror leans against the wall, and it shows the room more honestly than the room does.

They might have chosen to name the armour by its heraldry, or read its movement and walk around it — John Napier made the case: "Last one in buys the ale!" The party chose to open with a combat spell.

A bed of rusted spikes stands out of the floor, most of them still upright. A brazier burns in its bracket — nobody has been here to feed it, and it burns anyway.
