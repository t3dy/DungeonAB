The transcript

🌿 Room 1 — materials

A room of herbs, salts, and quicksilver — alchemy materials, free to gather. Somebody stacked supplies here and never came back for them. A boulder sits at the top of a slope, held by a wedge of rotten timber.

They might have chosen to gather the materials — Michael Sendivogius made the case: "The sword's just how I open lockboxes." The party chose to move on.

The party crosses the room and takes the far door.

👹 Room 2 — monster

A bone warden on its rounds holds the room (attack 7, health 18). The party must decide how to get past it. Plate and chitin cover it: the party's blows do 2 less damage each round. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. Shelves sag under books nobody has audited in a century. A gargoyle spout drips something that is not water into a stained channel.

They might have chosen to accept the duel, or negotiate the terms — Tycho Brahe made the case: "My shield has a saint on it. She's watching. Form up." The party chose to open with a combat spell.

🔥 The caster opens with Dawnbreak, chosen precisely for the monster's holy weakness: spell damage ×1.5. ⚔️ The party kills a bone warden on its rounds in 1 round, taking 0 damage. 🛡️ Aegis of Ash goes up before the first blow: 2 less damage every round. ✨ The working holds: +5 damage every round while the fight lasts. 🎒 3 more preparations hold besides — the ledger keeps them.

🛡️ The order settles with Michael Sendivogius at the head of it: whatever comes, comes to them.

⚠️ Room 3 — trap

A trap blocks the corridor. The party must disarm it, avoid it, or take the hit. Dead beetles ring one tile: a poison trap. Light damage now, lingering venom later unless a cleric cures it.

They might have chosen to search for a way around — Cornelius Agrippa made the case: "The body is a temple. Mine's a fortress." The party chose to push through and take the hit.

💥 The party pushes through the trap, taking 3 damage. The Craven spotted the tripwire first: 1 less damage. 🐍 Poison needles: the party will take 2 poison damage next room (no cleric to cure it).

🚶 The party looks into the barracks wing (bunkrooms and a picked-over weapon rack) and keeps to the main route.

🪜 Room 4 — stairs

A stair cut into the rock, going down. Cold air comes up it.

They might have chosen to take the stair down — John Napier made the case: "A fight you skip counts double." The party chose to camp at the stairhead first.

🏕️ The party makes camp at the stairhead and eats before the climb: 6 healed each, then down to the second floor.

🐍 The venom carried from the last room acts: 2 damage taken on the march.

👹 Room 5 — monster

A barrow shade, thin as smoke holds the room (attack 11, health 16). The party must decide how to get past it. It is ethereal: weapons do only 60% damage unless a cleric blesses the blades. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. Somebody stacked supplies here and never came back for them. A brazier burns in its bracket — nobody has been here to feed it, and it burns anyway.

They might have chosen to stand and fight, or fall back — Michael Sendivogius made the case: "Everything down here is carrying something. I collect." The party chose to open with a combat spell.

🕳️ The floor gives way — a hidden trapdoor. The party lands further down the same level, 2 rooms past where they were, taking 4 damage, and the rooms between go unlooted.

⚠️ Room 6 — trap

A trap blocks the corridor. The party must disarm it, avoid it, or take the hit. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around.

They might have chosen to push through and take the hit — Tycho Brahe made the case: "Danger pay. Emphasis on pay." The party chose to search for a way around.

Nothing here needs doing. The party walks on.

🐉 Room 7 — boss

The boss chamber. The Shrouded King in his broken throne-niche waits at its center (attack 17, health 46); killing it clears the dungeon. Plate and chitin cover it: the party's blows do 2 less damage each round. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. A bed of rusted spikes stands out of the floor, most of them still upright. A crack runs the width of the room, wide enough to matter and too wide to jump twice.

They might have chosen to stand and fight, or fall back — Tycho Brahe made the case: "The sword's just how I open lockboxes." The party chose to put it down the crack in the floor.
