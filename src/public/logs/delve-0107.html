The transcript

⚠️ Room 1 — trap

A trap blocks the corridor. The party must disarm it, avoid it, or take the hit. A tripwire runs up the wall to a bell: an alarm trap. Little damage, but the next monster will be warned (+2 attack). Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around.

They might have chosen to disarm the trap, or push through and take the hit — Pico della Mirandola made the case: "There's a cheaper way to do this. There always is." The party chose to bypass it with a utility spell.

The party crosses the room and takes the far door.

⚗️ Room 2 — lab

An alchemy lab with a working bench. An alchemist with materials can brew or coat weapons here. Shelves sag under books nobody has audited in a century. Somebody stacked supplies here and never came back for them.

They might have chosen to experiment with the assembly, or dissolve the lock — the Cunning looked for the way that costs least. The party chose to crack the crates open.

📦 The party cracks open stacked crates and barrels: 12 gold, 1 material.

🕯️ Room 3 — shrine

A shrine. Resting here heals the party; the gold leaf on the altar could be stripped instead. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind. A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong.

They might have chosen to attune the resonances, or endure the discord — the Cunning had already worked out the odds. The party chose to strip the gold leaf.

⛏️ The party strips 30 gold of leaf from the altar. The next disaster will hit harder for it.

🌿 Room 4 — materials

A room of herbs, salts, and quicksilver — alchemy materials, free to gather. A gargoyle spout drips something that is not water into a stained channel. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around.

They might have chosen to search the room methodically, or gather the materials — the Cunning picked the safer angle. The party chose to divine what is hidden here.

🔮 Divine sense finds what mortal eyes miss: a handful of coin scattered in crevices. +8 gold, +10 score.

🕳️ Robert Fludd finds a hidden door into the flooded wing. Behind it: a floor that slopes down into standing water. Its rooms join the route.

👹 Room 5 — monster

A crimson mist that pours under the door holds the room (attack 12, health 16). The party must decide how to get past it. It is ethereal: weapons do only 60% damage unless a cleric blesses the blades. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. Shelves sag under books nobody has audited in a century. A pit takes up a third of the floor. The bottom is not visible. An anvil sits under a dead forge, still true.

They might have chosen to stand and fight, or fall back — Simon Forman made the case: "The light goes first. I merely follow it, loudly." The party chose to turn the undead.

✨ The cleric turns the undead: a crimson mist that pours under the door crumbles. +30 score. 🌫️ A crimson mist that pours under the door drops a phial of settled red: a potion (heals 8), added to the satchel.

💎 Room 6 — vault

A hidden vault, stacked with treasure. Vaults always hold something beyond coin. A portcullis hangs raised above the passage, on a chain that still turns. Shelves sag under books nobody has audited in a century.

They might have chosen to loot the treasure, or inspect it first — the Cunning picked the safer angle. The party chose to open it with Knock.

🚪 Knock opens the lock at range: 121 gold taken. The noise carries through the dungeon.

🪜 Room 7 — stairs

A stair cut into the rock, going down. Cold air comes up it.

They might have chosen to take the stair down — the Cunning picked the safer angle. The party chose to rope down the shaft beside it.

🪜 The party ropes down the shaft beside the stair and lands on the second floor. 🪢 The line goes down the shaft beside the stair: the party descends without burning a march of oil.

🕯️ Someone checks the reservoir and does not like the answer: oil for 2 more marches.

📚 Room 8 — library

A library. The party can study here to learn spells. Shelves sag under books nobody has audited in a century.

They might have chosen to study the shelves, or move on — Pico della Mirandola made the case: "Why duel what you can outwit?" The party chose to read the sealed texts.

🔏 The wizard reads the sealed texts: 2 spells learned, including a forbidden working. +50 score.

🕯️ The wick is well down the oil. 1 more march of light, then none.

🪐 Room 9 — situation

A connecting corridor. Nothing blocks the way; the party moves through.

They might have chosen to accept the duel, or push past him — the Cunning had already worked out the odds. The party chose to recognize his school.

📖 The guard is Bolognese, the counter to it is on a page somebody in the party has read, and the bout lasts four seconds. He takes it well, and mentions what is waiting further down. +26 score, and the next snare is known.

🕯️ The flame stands up, thins, and is gone. The party is out of oil.

💰 Room 10 — treasure

A treasure chest sits in the room. It may hold gold; it may be a mimic. Somebody stacked supplies here and never came back for them.

They might have chosen to appraise the three chests, or read the maker's mark — the Cunning had already worked out the odds. The party chose to open it with Knock.

🚪 Knock opens the lock at range: 56 gold taken. The noise carries through the dungeon.

🪶 No light, but no stumbling either — Feather Step carries them through blind and whole, 3 damage unpaid.

🐉 Room 11 — boss

The boss chamber. The Vampire Lord, apologizing for the hour waits at its center (attack 22, health 59); killing it clears the dungeon. It is ethereal: weapons do only 60% damage unless a cleric blesses the blades. It will not face the cleric: undead take holy damage ×1.5. A silvered mirror leans against the wall, and it shows the room more honestly than the room does. A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong. A bed of rusted spikes stands out of the floor, most of them still upright. A portcullis hangs raised above the passage, on a chain that still turns.

They might have chosen to stand and fight, or fall back — Pico della Mirandola made the case: "Range is a suggestion. Watch." The party chose to open with a combat spell.
