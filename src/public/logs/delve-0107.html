⚠️ Room 1 — trap

A trap blocks the corridor. The party must disarm it, avoid it, or take the hit. A tripwire runs up the wall to a bell: an alarm trap. Little damage, but the next monster will be warned (+2 attack). A bed of rusted spikes stands out of the floor, most of them still upright. Half the ceiling is on the floor, in pieces worth stepping around.

They might have chosen to push through and take the hit, or search for a way around — John Dee made the case: "The god forgives. That's the whole strategy." The party chose to sift the rubble.

🪨 The party sifts a fall of rubble: 13 gold.

🛡️ John Napier walks in front, which is where the blows land first.

🪐 Room 2 — situation

A connecting corridor. Nothing blocks the way; the party moves through.

They might have chosen to experiment with the assembly, or dissolve the lock — John Napier made the case: "Last one in buys the ale!" The party chose to take one of the side passages.

🛤️ The side passage goes round, and round, and eventually through. Two more marches of oil burned than the direct road would have cost. +8 score.

🪐 Room 3 — situation

A connecting corridor. Nothing blocks the way; the party moves through.

They might have chosen to read the maker's mark, or pick the chest that was opened before — Cornelius Agrippa made the case: "I'll guard the rear. Someone has to. Far back." The party chose to appraise the three chests.

💰 Examined closely, the real chest is obvious — and so is how the other two were rigged, which is a lesson that keeps. +40 gold, +25 score, and the next snare is already understood. 🎓 Near enough to the discipline to try, not near enough to be paid for it: 10 renown short.

🕯️ Room 4 — shrine

A shrine. Resting here heals the party; the gold leaf on the altar could be stripped instead. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind. A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong.

They might have chosen to rest and heal, or move on — Michael Maier made the case: "Gold in, gold out — that's the whole science." The party chose to strip the gold leaf.

⛏️ The party strips 30 gold of leaf from the altar. The next disaster will hit harder for it.

The candles go out: the castle itself attacks the party in the dark. The party must brace together or scatter.

They might have chosen to brace together, or scatter and regroup — John Dee made the case: "The god counts. So do I." The party chose to sift the rubble.

🪨 The party sifts a fall of rubble: 13 gold.

A library. The party can study here to learn spells. Shelves sag under books nobody has audited in a century. Pillars break the room into aisles — something to fight behind.

They might have chosen to study the shelves — John Napier made the case: "Everything down here is carrying something. I collect." The party chose to move on.

The party crosses the room and takes the far door.

🕯️ The wick is well down the oil. 2 more marches of light, then none.

🕯️ The lantern is burning low: oil for 1 more march.

💰 Room 8 — treasure

A treasure chest sits in the room. It may hold gold; it may be a mimic. A silvered mirror leans against the wall, and it shows the room more honestly than the room does.

They might have chosen to search the room methodically, or divine what is hidden here — Michael Maier made the case: "Don't burn the carcass — half my income is in the glands." The party chose to observe every detail.

👁️ Hidden in plain sight: an alcove holding coin and small valuables, overlooked by a hundred hurrying parties. +15 gold, +18 score. 🎓 Done by people who do something near it: 10 renown less than a clean job.

🕯️ The flame stands up, thins, and is gone. The party is out of oil.

A sarcophagus stands against the wall with its lid slightly wrong. A bed of rusted spikes stands out of the floor, most of them still upright.
